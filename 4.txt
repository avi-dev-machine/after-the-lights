চতুর্থী — For Someone, Somewhere

Choturthi was spent at home.

One of those days when you don’t really want to go out,
but staying in feels strangely worse.
The hours pass, the phone lights up,
and somehow, none of it feels like enough.

Outside, Kolkata was getting ready.
The para was brighter than yesterday,
the pandals a little closer to completion,
and the evenings carried that familiar promise
of nights that wouldn’t end early.

I should have been excited.
I was, in a way.
But beneath all that anticipation
was a quieter feeling I couldn’t shake.

I missed something.
Or perhaps someone.

The difficult part was not knowing
whether I missed a person,
a moment that hadn’t happened yet,
or simply the feeling of being somewhere
I was genuinely wanted.

I kept checking my phone,
sometimes without even realising it.
A new message, a familiar name,
a conversation that might last longer than expected—
small things, really,
but that afternoon,
I found myself hoping for them.

It’s funny how longing works.
It doesn’t always arrive as a grand confession.
Sometimes it is just an empty evening
in which one particular person’s absence
feels louder than everything else.

I wondered what the next few days would bring.
The crowded streets, the late-night walks,
the endless “kothay achish?” messages,
the photographs we’d take without thinking,
the conversations we’d wish had lasted longer.

And somewhere in those imagined moments,
there was a quiet hope
of running into someone unexpectedly,
of having a little more time,
of finding a reason to take the longer way home.

I didn’t know if any of that would happen.
Perhaps that was what made the waiting so difficult.

By evening, the neighbourhood had grown louder.
Somewhere, a dhaak was being tested.
People were stepping out in fresh clothes,
making plans, finding their people.

I watched the day fade from my window
and wondered whether someone, somewhere,
was thinking about me, too.

Not necessarily in the same way.
Not necessarily at all.

But there is a particular ache
in hoping to cross someone’s mind
without knowing if you ever do.

And maybe I was missing home, too—
not just the place,
but the version of myself that existed there,
the familiar faces, the effortless laughter,
the feeling that I didn’t have to arrange
every little moment of happiness myself.

Choturthi gave me no story to tell,
only a few thoughts I couldn’t quite settle.
The kind that return when the room goes quiet
and you have nothing left to distract yourself with.

The city was almost ready for Pujo.
I was waiting for it, too.

For the lights, the crowds, the familiar chaos.
For the people I hadn’t seen in too long.
For a few moments I couldn’t plan or promise.

And, if I’m being honest,
for someone I couldn’t quite admit
I was hoping to see.
