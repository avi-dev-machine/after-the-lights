পঞ্চমী — The Day Finally Began

The morning went into a viva,
a presentation, a few questions
I hope I answered well enough,
and the usual relief of being done with it.

Pujo could wait, apparently.
The college had other plans.

But by evening,
the laptop was away,
the formal answers were forgotten,
and we were somewhere in South Kolkata,
trying to decide which pandal to visit next.

One person wanted to go this way,
another was sure there was a better one nearby.
Someone stopped for pictures,
someone complained about the crowd,
and somehow we all kept walking together.

The lights were on,
the streets were full,
and every other corner had something going on.
We ate whatever looked good,
took a few pictures,
and talked about absolutely nothing important.

Honestly, the best part was
not having to think about anything else.

No deadlines for a few hours,
no viva questions replaying in my head,
just familiar faces and a city
that felt a little more alive than usual.

We didn’t cover every pandal.
We didn’t need to.

There were still places left to see,
food left to try,
and plenty of arguments about
where we should go tomorrow.

Panchami, more or less—
a stressful morning,
a good evening,
and another reason to be glad
that Pujo comes around every year.

Some days don’t need a deeper meaning.
It’s enough that they happened,
and that you were there for them.
