নবমী — Five Years, Give or Take

Five years.

That’s how long it had been since we’d properly sat down
and spent an evening together.

From school corridors to wherever life had taken us,
there’d been a lot in between.
Conversations that stopped,
differences that stayed a little too long,
arguments neither of us probably remembers
in exactly the same way.

We weren’t always on the best terms.
There were times we barely spoke,
times when I wondered if we’d ever go back
to being the friends we used to be.

And then, somehow, it was Navami,
and we were sitting across from each other again.

Over whiskey and beer,
we started catching up on five years
as if there weren’t five years to catch up on.

One story led to another.
Some things had changed completely.
Some things, hilariously, hadn’t changed at all.

The same old humour.
The same instinct to pull each other’s leg.
The same laughter that starts with a stupid joke
and somehow leaves both of you gasping for breath.

There were stories we hadn’t heard,
things that had happened while we weren’t around,
and conversations that probably should have happened
a long time ago.

Later, at Maddox,
with the familiar Pujo crowd moving around us,
we sat and laughed our hearts out.

No grand reconciliation.
No dramatic speech about how much we’d missed each other.
Just two people who’d known each other since school,
slowly filling in the gaps.

I think there’s something comforting
about meeting an old friend after years.

You don’t have to pretend the past was perfect.
You don’t have to agree about everything
that happened between you.
You can acknowledge that things went wrong
and still be glad that you’re sitting there now.

Maybe growing up has something to do with that—
understanding that people can drift apart
without becoming strangers forever.

Some friendships don’t pick up
exactly where they left off.
They find a different way forward,
with a little more history,
a few more stories,
and hopefully a little more understanding.

By the time the evening was over,
five years felt both impossibly long
and surprisingly small.

We’d missed a lot of each other’s lives.
There were things we’d never get to know firsthand,
moments we couldn’t go back and share.

But we had that evening.
The drinks, the adda, the laughter at Maddox,
and all those stories finally finding their way out.

Navami reminded me that not every reunion
needs to make up for lost time.

Sometimes, it’s enough to sit across from someone
who knew you before you became who you are now,
laugh about the years in between,
and realise there’s still a friendship worth keeping.

And honestly,
it’s good to have an old friend back.
