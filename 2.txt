দ্বিতীয়া — The Days Before We Disappear

My days have become a collection of unfinished things.

An assignment due at midnight,
a project that refuses to cooperate,
a dozen tabs open on my laptop,
and a head full of thoughts
that have very little to do with what I’m studying.

The semester is in full swing.
Pujo, apparently, is not an acceptable excuse
for a deadline.

Meanwhile, my friends are discussing plans.
Someone has bought new clothes,
someone is making a list of pandals,
and someone has already decided
where we’ll eat on Saptami.

I read the messages between two tasks
and find myself smiling at the screen.

It’s strange how a few ordinary conversations
can make a tiring day feel lighter.

Perhaps that’s what I’m looking forward to most—
not the grand celebrations,
but the familiar chaos of being together.

The arguments over where to go,
the friend who takes an hour to get ready,
the photographs nobody likes at first,
the laughter that gets louder
the less sense the joke makes.

The sort of happiness
that doesn’t require a special occasion,
except that, somehow, it always finds one.

Though I suppose growing up has changed things.

We have our own schedules now,
our own worries tucked behind casual replies,
our own little worlds that don’t always overlap.

Still, when we finally make plans,
there is a comfort in knowing
that some things haven’t become unfamiliar.

And maybe that’s enough.

Lately, I’ve been thinking about how easily
the ordinary parts of life pass unnoticed—
the people we speak to every day,
the friends whose presence we take for granted,
the small moments that never seem important
until we look back on them.

I don’t want to turn every happy evening
into something sentimental.

I just want to be there for it.

To laugh without thinking about tomorrow,
to listen when someone has a story to tell,
to walk through the city with nowhere particular to be.

And perhaps to notice
when someone’s company feels a little different,
when a conversation stays with me
long after we’ve said goodbye.

Some feelings arrive without an introduction.
They settle quietly into the spaces
between ordinary things.

I haven’t decided what to call this one.

For now, there are assignments to finish,
a project to make sense of,
and a Pujo plan that keeps growing
with every message in the group chat.

Life carries on in its usual mess.

But somewhere in the middle of it,
I find myself looking forward to these days
with a happiness I can’t quite explain.

Not because everything is falling into place,
but because, beyond the work and the noise,
there are people I want to see,
stories I want to be part of,
and moments I haven’t lived yet.

Maybe growing up isn’t about finding more time for life.
Maybe it’s about recognising the moments
that make all the hard work feel worthwhile.
