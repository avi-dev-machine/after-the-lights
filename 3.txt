তৃতীয়া — Before the Pandals Open

The pandals aren’t open yet,
the bamboo frames are still taking shape,
and the city is somewhere between
its everyday self and the madness of Pujo.

But the evenings have started feeling different.

There’s a little more traffic near the para,
a little more excitement in the group chat,
and that familiar restlessness
that makes staying in feel like a waste of a perfectly good evening.

So we went to Triptis.

A table, a couple of beers,
cholabhaja dusted with masala,
thin slices of ada kuchi,
and gossip that had absolutely no business
being as entertaining as it was.

Someone brought up an old scene.
Someone else remembered it differently.
Names were dropped, theories were proposed,
and every few minutes,
the entire table dissolved into laughter.

“না না, পুরোটা শোন আগে!”

Of course, nobody ever gets to tell the whole story.

Outside, a yellow taxi honked impatiently.
A bus groaned past the crossing.
Somewhere nearby, a tea stall was still doing business,
and the evening carried that familiar Kolkata mix
of cigarette smoke, street food, and conversations
that refuse to end when they should.

We spoke about work,
complained about assignments,
briefly remembered that projects were still pending,
and then went straight back to gossip.

There was Pujo planning, naturally.

Which pandal first?
Who was buying new clothes?
Who would inevitably be late?
And who would disappear into the crowd
only to reappear asking where everyone was?

The pandals weren’t ready,
but the group had already started falling into place.

And maybe that’s the nicest part of these evenings—
the unremarkable rituals before the big celebration,
when nobody needs a perfect photograph
or an extraordinary plan.

Just a table full of familiar faces,
a plate of cholabhaja that empties too quickly,
one more round,
and the easy comfort of being among people
who remember your old stories
and give you new reasons to laugh.

Soon, the city will be crowded with lights,
the dhaak will take over the nights,
and every street will seem to lead somewhere worth going.

For now, though,
there’s adda at Triptis,
ada kuchi between sips,
and the ridiculous feeling
that we’ve somehow managed to make an ordinary evening
the first proper memory of Pujo.

Ma hasn’t arrived yet,
the pandals are still unfinished,
but somewhere between the gossip and the laughter,
the season has already begun to feel like ours.
