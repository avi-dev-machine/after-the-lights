সপ্তমী — An Unexpected Kind of Pujo

Saptami wasn’t supposed to be anything special.

It started with a sudden afternoon plan,
an old friend I hadn’t spent enough time with lately,
and a bunch of people I’d never met before.

Her college friends.
Names I’d forget, faces I was still figuring out,
conversations I wasn’t sure how to enter.

I almost didn’t know what I’d signed up for.

But somewhere along the way,
the awkwardness wore off.
The jokes started landing,
the afternoon stretched into evening,
and these unfamiliar people
began feeling a little less unfamiliar.

Then it rained.

Not the kind of rain you wait for
with a cup of cha and a window to look out of.
The kind that catches you halfway through a plan,
soaks your clothes, ruins your hair,
and makes everyone run for the nearest shade.

And there we were.

Sitting beside each other,
watching the rain come down,
with nowhere particular to go
and no real reason to get up.

I don’t remember what we were talking about
for most of that time.
Only the little things.

The way the conversation kept going.
The ease of sitting next to someone
you’d only just started getting to know.
The cigarettes from the counter,
passed from my hand,
and those small, unremarkable seconds
that somehow didn’t feel unremarkable at all.

At some point, we ran through the rain again.
Because apparently, getting drenched once
wasn’t enough for us.

There was a gully, too.
One of those narrow Kolkata lanes
where the Pujo noise feels a little distant
and the rest of the world seems to carry on
somewhere at the other end.

For a little while, it was just the two of us there.

No big moment.
No dramatic confession.
Just a conversation, a pause,
and that strange awareness
of someone being there beside you.

I’d met plenty of people before.
I’d had good conversations,
laughed with strangers,
spent entire evenings with people
I never thought about again.

This felt a little different.

I couldn’t tell you exactly when it started.
Maybe under the shade while it rained.
Maybe in that gully.
Maybe in the way we kept finding
another reason to continue the conversation.

Or maybe I was just noticing
what I usually let pass me by.

We exchanged numbers before the day was over.
Such a small thing, really.
Two people saving each other’s contacts,
something that happens every day.

Still, I went home with a little hope
that this wouldn’t be the last conversation.

And somewhere in the back of my mind
was that October theory—
the idea that October brings unexpected people,
new beginnings, or feelings
you weren’t looking for in the first place.

I don’t know if I believe in theories like that.
Perhaps we give ordinary coincidences
meaning because we want them to mean something.

But it was Saptami.
The city was full of lights,
the streets were wet,
and for once, I wasn’t thinking about
how quickly Pujo would be over.

I was thinking about someone I’d met that afternoon.

Wondering if she’d felt it too.
If she’d replayed any of those little moments.
If the conversation had stayed with her
the way it had stayed with me.

I knew almost nothing about her.
Not enough to call it anything.
Not enough to know where it might go.

And perhaps that was the nicest part.

For once, there was no history to untangle,
no expectations to live up to,
no old story waiting to repeat itself.

Just a new person,
a handful of moments,
and a feeling I hadn’t quite learnt
how to name.

Maybe it was attraction.
Maybe it was curiosity.
Maybe it was the beginning of attachment,
that dangerous little habit of looking forward
to someone you have no reason to expect anything from.

All I knew was that an afternoon
I hadn’t planned to remember
had become the one I kept returning to.

Pujo has a way of making the city feel magical.
But that Saptami, somewhere between the rain,
the smoke, the narrow lanes and a new number saved on my phone,
the magic felt a little more personal.

For the first time in a long time,
I wasn’t just hoping to make good memories this Pujo.

I was hoping one of them
might turn into something more.

And I had no idea
whether October would let it.
