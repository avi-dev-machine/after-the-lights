প্রথমা — Before It All Begins

There is a certain tenderness to Sarat,
a softness the rest of the year forgets—
the blue of a sky that seems almost endless,
the kaash flowers swaying beside the roads,
the shiuli falling before the world wakes up.

And somewhere, in the heart of Kolkata,
the first dhaak begins to sound.

The pandals are still unfinished,
Ma has not yet opened her eyes,
and the city is only beginning
to remember how to be happy.

Yet something inside me has already changed.

Perhaps it is the thought of home,
of familiar voices filling up the rooms,
of evenings that ask nothing of us
except that we stay a little longer.

Or perhaps it is the knowledge
that these days, too, will pass.

That soon, the streets will fill with laughter,
the nights will grow impossibly beautiful,
and before I have learnt to hold them close,
I will be counting the hours until goodbye.

Growing up is such a quiet thing.

One day, you are waiting for Pujo
because of the new clothes and the pandals.
Years later, you find yourself waiting
for something you cannot quite name—
a feeling of belonging,
a face you hope to see,
a reason to believe
that not everything beautiful has to leave.

And perhaps that is the beauty of Prathama— nothing has happened yet, and somehow, my heart is already full.

The city is beginning to feel like home again, the familiar faces are finding their way back, and somewhere in the distance, a dhaak rehearses the happiness we have been waiting all year to feel.

I want to walk these streets without watching the time, to laugh at things I won’t remember tomorrow, to find little moments I never planned for and wish, just once, that they could last.

Because somewhere in me, I know that one day these evenings will become memories, these familiar faces will be miles away, and I will miss the very life I am living so carelessly today.

But for now, autumn has only just begun— and I have not yet run out of reasons to fall in love with it.
